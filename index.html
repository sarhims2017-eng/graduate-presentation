Effect of Dietary Habits During Ramadan on Premenstrual Syndrome

A Cross-Sectional Study


Presented by: Talen Bugis, Layan Khayyat, Sara Alsayed, Amera ALsulimani

Supervised by: Dr. Walaa Essam Alhassani

Umm Al-Qura University - Clinical Nutrition Department

Background

PMS

Impacts emotional & physical well-being of reproductive-age women.

Ramadan

Drastic changes: fasting, late-night wakefulness, altered diet.

Dietary Shifts

High intake of fried foods, sweets, and caffeine at Iftar/Suhoor.

General Evidence: Extensive global literature consistently links daily dietary habits (e.g., high caffeine, processed fats) and poor sleep to PMS severity, acting as independent modulators even outside of Ramadan.
(References: Siminiuc & Turcanu (2023), Hashim et al. (2019), and Mighani et al. (2025))

Problem & Significance

  • The Gap: Specific impact of Ramadan habits on PMS among young women in Makkah remains unexplored.
  • The Need: Clinicians lack culturally appropriate evidence.
  • Significance: Provides evidence to help dietitians offer tailored recommendations.

Objectives & Hypotheses

To investigate the relationship between Ramadan lifestyle changes and PMS severity:


Dietary Habits

Sleep Quality

Physical Activity

BMI
Hypothesis: We hypothesize that high consumption of fried foods and caffeine, along with poor sleep quality during Ramadan, will be positively correlated with an increase in PMS severity.
(Based on Mighani et al., 2025 & Siminiuc & Turcanu, 2023)

Results: Baseline & BMI

Mean Age: 23.14 ± 6.10 years

Students: 72.7%

Normal BMI: 59.5%

Results: Dietary Habits

High prevalence of caffeine (43%) and fried food (33.1%) intake during Ramadan may contribute to increased PMS severity.

Association: Caffeine & PMS

Clear increase in PMS severity with caffeine consumption (p < 0.001)

Note: Fried Food also showed a moderate significant association (p = 0.046).

Sleep & Depression vs PMS

Strong positive association: Both poorer sleep (PSQI) and higher depression (PHQ-9) strongly correlate with severe PMS (p < 0.001).

Results: Physical Activity (IPAQ)

60% of participants were strictly inactive during Ramadan. No significant association with PMS severity was found (p > 0.05).
* Note: Analyzed sample size for physical activity is n=105 (after rigorous data cleaning).

Discussion

  • Interpretation: Findings suggest that caffeine, fried food, and poor sleep are associated with increased PMS severity through hormonal and inflammatory mechanisms.
  • Diet & Sleep: High fried food, caffeine, and poor sleep exacerbated PMS, aligning with Siminiuc & Turcanu (2023) and Mighani et al. (2025).
  • Physical Activity Insight: The lack of association may be attributed to our imbalanced sample size, as 60% of our respondents reported being strictly inactive during Ramadan.

Critique

Strengths

First culturally specific baseline data exploring Ramadan lifestyle alterations & PMS in the Makkah region.

Limitations

Cross-sectional design limits causality.
Self-reported data may introduce recall bias.
Possible confounding variables (e.g., stress).
Reverse causation cannot be excluded.

Clinical Practice Application

Healthcare providers and dietitians must utilize these findings to offer culturally tailored education.

Focus should be placed on managing caffeine limits, reducing deep-fried foods, and enforcing good sleep hygiene to help women manage PMS during the holy month.

Conclusion & Future Work

  • Conclusion: Unique lifestyle shifts in Ramadan are significantly associated with increased PMS severity and depression levels.
  • Future Directions (Based on our findings):
    • Conduct longitudinal studies to track patients before, during, and after Ramadan.
    • Utilize objective clinical measurements to overcome self-reporting limitations.
