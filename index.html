Effect of Dietary Habits During Ramadan on Premenstrual Syndrome

A Cross-Sectional Study among Females in Makkah

Presented by: Talen Bugis, Layan Khayyat, Sara Alsayed, Amera ALsulimani

Supervised by: Dr. Walaa Essam Alhassani

UQU - Clinical Nutrition Department

Background

Dietary Impact: Modifiable factors like fats/caffeine are core PMS modulators.

Ramadan: Drastic shifts in meal timing (Iftar/Suhoor) and sleep-wake cycles.

Complexity: PMS affects emotional/physical well-being globally.

Zendehdel et al. (2017); Modzelewski et al. (2024); Faris et al. (2020)

Methodology: Design

Target

Females (18-40) in Makkah

Inclusion

Regular Menstrual Cycle

Exclusion

Pregnancy / Hormonal Meds

Final N

121 Participants

* Ethical Approval: Obtained from UQU Biomedical Ethics Committee.

Validated Tools

DRSP

PMS Severity (Arabic Validated)

PSQI

Sleep Quality (Global Standard)

PHQ-9

Depression (Clinically Proven)

FFQ

Ramadan-specific Dietary Recall

IPAQ-SF

Physical Activity METs

Rationale: Using gold-standard, Arabic-validated tools ensures reliability & cultural relevance (Mahfoud et al., 2018).

Results: Physical Activity

Findings: 60% Inactive. No significant link to PMS.

Interpretation: Fasting lethargy and the cultural shift to night-time rest likely suppressed activity levels, limiting statistical variance (n=105).

Discussion

  • Key Link: Fried foods and caffeine directly exacerbate PMS symptoms.
  • Biological Path: High-fat diets may trigger systemic inflammation (Prostaglandins).
  • Sleep Triad: Poor sleep strongly correlates with both PMS and depression (p < 0.001).
  • Short Window: Healthy food impact may need >1 month to show biological effects.
Siminiuc & Turcanu (2023); Mighani et al. (2025)

Critique

Strengths

First culturally-specific study in Makkah exploring Ramadan's unique lifestyle/PMS intersection.

Limitations
  • Cross-sectional (No causality)
  • Self-reported (Recall bias)
  • Small sample (121)
